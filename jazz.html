Jazz piano in a year

Five things to practise every day, with one exercise for each at your level, a year to work through all 12 keys, and a way to tell when you've passed each one. Based on the free lesson The Jazz Piano 1-Year Practice Plan by Piano With Jonny. Every exercise here plays for you, so you can hear it before you try it.

Today's practice

    How fast to move

    Every day

    Start at the tempo you reached last time. Play the exercise twice in a row: if both run-throughs are clean, go 4 BPM faster; if not, go 4 BPM slower and try again. Each exercise remembers your tempo, and its timer walks you through the minutes.

    Every week

    Each month has four steps. Week 1: learn the notes slowly, at about 60% of the goal tempo. Week 2: hands together without stopping (75%). Week 3: push the tempo (90%). Week 4: play it at the goal tempo. Every exercise shows this month's tempos and highlights the week you're in.

    Every month

    When you've practised all month and played at the goal tempo twice in a row, tick both boxes and finish the month. Behind? Stay on the key for another week; a year of 13 or 14 months is fine. Ahead? Keep the month, and use the spare minutes to review earlier keys.

    The video sets the daily time, one key a month and the goal tempos. The weekly steps and the 4 BPM rule are this page's suggestion for getting there.

    Play along with a MIDI keyboard, your computer keys or the on-screen piano. Computer keys: A to ; are the white keys, W E T Y U O P the black keys, Z / X shift the octave. In the examples, green keys are the right hand and blue keys the left hand.

    1

    Scales

    Scales are the harmonic language of jazz: the chords and the melodies of a tune all come from parent scales. Learn the scales and everything else gets easier.

    2

    Chords

    The rich chords of jazz are simple chords with notes added. Each level builds on the one before: triads, then seventh chords, then extensions and alterations.

    3

    Voicings

    A voicing is the way you lay a chord out across your hands. The same chord can sound plain or very jazzy depending on which notes you keep, which you drop and where you put them.

    4

    Lead sheets

    A lead sheet gives you only the melody and the chord symbols. Jazz players turn that raw material into their own arrangement, and this pillar is where everything else comes together. Learn one new tune a month from a fake book.

    5

    Improvisation

    You don't have to be advanced to solo. Each level gives you one idea to practise over a chord progression. Press Start to hear the band, then play over it: the tool listens and tells you how it went.

    Twelve months, twelve keys

    Keep the routine short and daily: 5–10 minutes on each pillar is enough. Watch the original lesson to see and hear every exercise played.
